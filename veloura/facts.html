Did You Know?

Car Facts

Short pieces of history, engineering and trivia from more than a century of luxury car making.

Rolls-Royce

The Ghost nameplate honours the 1906 Silver Ghost, a car whose reliability record helped build the company's early reputation.

Lamborghini

Ferruccio Lamborghini built tractors before he built sports cars, and the company still exists today as a separate tractor manufacturer.

Ferrari

Ferrari's first car under its own name was the 125 S of 1947, powered by a 1.5-litre V12.

Porsche

Porsche first used the Turbo badge on the 930 in 1975; the same badge now appears on fully electric Porsche models, where nothing is turbocharged at all.

Bentley

Bentley won the 24 Hours of Le Mans five times between 1924 and 1930, driven by the group of wealthy British racers nicknamed the Bentley Boys.

McLaren

The McLaren F1 of 1992 seated its driver in the centre, ahead of two passenger seats, and its engine bay was lined with gold foil for heat reflection.

Aston Martin

Aston Martin took its name from the Aston Hill hill climb, where co-founder Lionel Martin raced.

Mercedes-Benz

Karl Benz patented the Benz Patent-Motorwagen in 1886, generally recognised as the first practical automobile.

Engineering

Carbon fibre tubs, now common on supercars, came from Formula One: McLaren introduced the first carbon fibre F1 chassis in 1981.

Design

Active aerodynamics let modern supercars have it both ways, keeping drag low on a straight road and raising a wing for downforce only when cornering or braking.
