Overview

The second-generation Ghost, launched in 2020, is Rolls-Royce's smaller and more driver-focused saloon, positioned below the Phantom. It uses the aluminium spaceframe architecture shared with the Phantom and Cullinan, and a 6.75-litre twin-turbocharged V12.

Design

Rolls-Royce describes the styling approach as post-opulence: the surfaces are almost completely free of decoration, with a single hand-polished line running the length of the body. The grille is illuminated, and the doors can open and close under power.

Performance

The V12 delivers 850 Nm from low revs, which is what gives the Ghost its effortless character rather than outright speed. Top speed is limited to 250 km/h, and 0-100 km/h takes about 4.8 seconds despite the car's size.

Technology

A camera reads the road ahead so the suspension can prepare for bumps before the wheels reach them, a system Rolls-Royce calls the Flagbearer. The gearbox uses GPS data to pre-select the right gear for the road ahead, and all-wheel steering improves low-speed manoeuvrability.

History

The Ghost nameplate honours the Silver Ghost of 1906, the car that first established the company's reputation for reliability. The first modern Ghost appeared in 2009; the second generation arrived in 2020 and was updated as the Series II in 2024.

Interesting Facts

  • Rolls-Royce added 100 kg of sound insulation, then discovered the cabin felt unnervingly silent and had to tune some sound back in.
  • The Starlight headliner uses hundreds of fibre-optic lights set into the roof lining.
  • The Spirit of Ecstasy mascot retracts into the bonnet when the car is parked.
