Bentley Continental GT Speed

A hand-trimmed grand tourer that swapped its W12 for a more powerful hybrid V8.

Overview

The fourth-generation Continental GT Speed, revealed in 2024, retired Bentley's long-serving W12 engine in favour of a plug-in hybrid system built around a 4.0-litre twin-turbo V8. Combined output rose to 782 PS and 1,000 Nm, more than the W12 it replaced.

Design

The car keeps the Continental's familiar long bonnet and short rear deck, now with single round headlights instead of the previous four-lamp arrangement. Interiors are trimmed at Bentley's Crewe factory, where veneers and leather hides are prepared and fitted largely by hand.

Performance

Bentley quotes 0-100 km/h in 3.2 seconds and a top speed of 335 km/h. The electric motor fills the gaps in delivery at low revs, so the car pulls hard long before the turbochargers are fully spooled.

Technology

A 25.9 kWh battery gives a useful electric-only range for shorter journeys, and the car can be driven in full EV mode in town. A 48-volt active anti-roll system keeps the heavy body flat through corners.

History

The original Continental GT launched in 2003 and transformed Bentley from a low-volume manufacturer into a global luxury brand. The nameplate itself traces back to the R-Type Continental of 1952.

Interesting Facts

  • Bentley won the 24 Hours of Le Mans five times between 1924 and 1930.
  • A single Continental GT interior can use the hides of several cattle, selected for flawless grain.
  • The 2024 Speed is the most powerful road-going Bentley the company has built.
